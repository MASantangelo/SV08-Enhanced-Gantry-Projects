## Fasteners - Threaded Rods

| Qty | Part | Buy | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 2 | M3 Internally Threaded Spacer (5mm OD \| 35mm Tall) | [Amazon](https://amazon.com/dp/B0D3HWQ2GF) | Replaces the M5 x 40mm dowel in the XY Joints (1 per joint). Clamped between the plates by 2x M3 x 10mm Ultra Thin Flat Head per joint. FIT NOTE: the spacers measure about 5.05 mm across (anodised aluminium), a little too big for the 4.992-5.000 mm bore of a 695 bearing, so sand the whole length of each spacer down to fit, not just the ends: the toothed pulley or idler has to slide along the shaft to reach its place between the bearings, and sanding only the last 5 mm of each end isn't enough. This is common practice in the 3D printing community, and it's confirmed working on the v2 test build. Spin the spacer in a drill against 400-600 grit, or roll it under a flat block on sandpaper laid on the bench. Measure in two directions as you go (aim for about 4.990-4.995 mm), and stop when it presses into a bearing with firm thumb pressure and the pulley slides over it. Don't force an oversize spacer in; that loads the bearing balls. ALTERNATIVE: a 5 mm dowel pin, 40 mm long, works in place of the spacer and its two flat-head screws. At 40 mm it runs the full width of both bearings, flush with their outer faces. Get h6/h7 (slip-fit) dowels or a cut length of 5 mm ground linear shaft (h6/g6): standard m6 press-fit dowels are 5.004-5.012 mm and will jam in the bearing just like the oversize spacers. Test-fit each dowel in a bearing before assembly and sand or grind it to size the same way if it's tight. The pulley's set screws hold the dowel in place, and the pulley sits between the two bearings. |
